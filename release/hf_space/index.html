Key Finding & Normalisation Caveat:

While histopathology foundation models achieve seemingly high correlation (r ≈ 0.51) under raw log1p targets, this predictability is almost entirely driven by spot cellularity (cell density). After standard library-size normalisation (lognorm), foundation models (r ≈ 0.167) barely outperform a scalar cell-count baseline (r ≈ 0.158) and an 8-cell-type composition oracle (r ≈ 0.137).

Phase 3: Spatial Context Ablation

Comparing single-patch histology embeddings (center 112 µm) against pooled embeddings aggregating immediate hexagonal spatial neighbor rings (+k1 = 6 neighbors, radius ~112 µm; +k2 = 18 neighbors) and learned attention pooling.

Macro-Average Pearson r by Target

Context Architecture lognorm (Normalised) log1p (Raw Counts)
Virchow2 Single Patch (112 µm)0.1670.510
Virchow2 + k=1 Ring (6 neighbors)0.178 (+0.011)0.514 (+0.004)
Virchow2 + k=2 Rings (18 neighbors)0.184 (+0.017)0.513 (+0.003)
Virchow2 Attention (k=1)0.171 (+0.004)0.485 (-0.025)

Key Takeaways

  • Under raw log1p: Expanding spatial context provides minimal gain (+0.004) because spot cellularity (tissue density) is already primarily localized to the central patch.
  • Under normalised lognorm: Adding spatial rings consistently improves prediction (0.167 → 0.178 → 0.184). When total cellularity is normalised, surrounding morphological architecture helps resolve cell states and microenvironmental niches.
  • Attention pooling: Learned attention on frozen embeddings does not outperform uniform mean-pooling across neighbor rings.
