## Methods in plain language

- **Pseudo-spots.** Xenium measures single transcripts. We sum the transcripts of all cells whose centre lies in
  the same square as each H&E image patch, giving one expression vector per patch.
- **Targets.** `log1p` is log(1 + count). `lognorm` first divides each spot's counts by its total count (and
  rescales), then takes the log. Dividing by the total removes most of the effect of how many cells the spot holds.
- **Model.** Each image patch goes through a frozen foundation model. The embedding is standardised, reduced to
  256 dimensions with PCA fitted on training data only, and one ridge regression per gene predicts expression.
- **Folds.** Leave-one-group-out: train on all groups but one, test on the held-out group. Samples that may come
  from the same patient or block (NCBI887/NCBI888) share a group.
- **Metric.** Pearson r between predicted and measured expression across test spots, per gene, then averaged.
- **Baselines.** The cell count is the number of Xenium-segmented cells in the spot. It comes from molecular data,
  so it is a reference point, not an image-only competitor. Composition is the fraction of each marker-based cell
  type; the Leiden baseline uses unsupervised clusters fitted on training samples only.
- **Paired comparison.** For each held-out group we take (image r - baseline r) averaged over genes, then bootstrap
  over groups for a 95% interval. Genes are not independent, so per-gene p-values are not used as evidence.
- **Moran's I** measures how spatially smooth a gene's expression is. **Tau** measures how specific a gene is to one
  cell type.

## Likely reviewer questions (honest answers)

1. **Only 9 samples?** Yes; these are the human Xenium 5K samples with H&E available in HEST-1k when
   the study was run. Intervals are wide and per-organ conclusions are weak.
2. **Is the cell-count baseline fair?** It uses segmentation from the molecular data, which an image model does not
   see. It shows how much of the signal a cell count alone carries; an H&E nuclei count was not tested.
3. **Is lognorm the right target?** It is one standard choice. Other normalisations (e.g. SCTransform) were not tested.
4. **Why linear probes?** This follows the HEST protocol. A one-hidden-layer MLP gave similar results
   (ridge 0.477 vs MLP 0.484, raw counts, gene subset). Fine-tuning was not tested.
5. **Is the result new?** Normalisation and cell-count effects are well known in spatial transcriptomics. What we add
   is a transcriptome-wide comparison of foundation models against these baselines on both targets. Check
   `paper/related_work_candidates.md` and the literature yourself before claiming novelty.
6. **Leakage?** Splits are group-level with unit tests; PCA, ridge alpha and clustering are fitted on training data
   only; the permutation control gives r = -0.000.
7. **Do some genes beat the baselines?** Yes, e.g. fibroblast-associated CTHRC1, THY1, ADAM12 on normalised targets
   (`reports/phase2/lognorm/gene_table.csv`).
8. **Spatial context?** A small mean gain on normalised targets (0.010); attention pooling did not help.
9. **Cross-platform?** Tested on lung only (Visium -> Xenium).
10. **Why is cell typing coarse?** Marker typing left most cells unassigned; the Leiden baseline was added to check
    this and did not change the conclusion.
